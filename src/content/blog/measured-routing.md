There's a reflex in AI engineering to reach for the biggest model available and move on. It
feels safe. It's also frequently wrong — and it's expensive in latency, memory, and cost.

## The result that started it

We ran four local models through GSM8K — 200 grade-school math problems, exact-match grading —
on an Apple-Silicon machine via Ollama. Here's what came back:

| Model | Accuracy | 95% CI | RAM |
|---|---|---|---|
| phi3 (3.8B) | **83.0%** | ±5.2% | 3.85 GB |
| qwen2.5:3b | 82.0% | ±5.3% | 2.16 GB |
| llama3:8b | 79.0% | ±5.6% | 5.02 GB |
| gemma2:2b | 59.0% | ±6.8% | 1.93 GB |

The 8B model is **not** the most accurate here. A 3.8B model beats it by four points while
using a quarter less RAM. If you'd routed by parameter count, you'd have picked the slower,
heavier, *less accurate* option — and paid for the privilege.

## Why priors fail

Vendor benchmark tables describe *their* data on *their* harness. Your tasks are not that
data. Difficulty, phrasing, domain, and your grading criteria all shift the ranking. The only
way to know which model is good enough for *your* workload is to measure it on *your* workload.

That's a feedback loop, and most stacks don't have one — they route on a static guess and
never revisit it.

## How Aether closes the loop

Aether treats measured quality as a first-class input to compilation:

1. **Evaluate.** `aether evaluate` scores models on a dataset with deterministic grading and
   writes per-task measured quality, tagged with provenance `meas`.
2. **Overlay.** Those measurements overlay the model registry, replacing estimated priors
   (`est`) wherever real data exists.
3. **Route.** The next time you compile, the routing pipeline filters by your policy — cost,
   quality, latency, privacy — and scores candidates using *your* measured numbers.

The point isn't to always pick the smallest model. It's to pick the smallest model that
**clears your quality floor** — and to know, with a confidence interval, that it does.

> Measured, not guessed. Reliability decisions should be backed by reproducible numbers on
> your data, not by a vendor's slide or a parameter count.

Everything above is reproducible — the benchmarks are generated by `aether evaluate`, not
hand-written. See the [benchmark section](/aether#benchmarks) on the Aether page, or the
[optimization pillar](/docs#optimization) for how the feedback loop is wired.
